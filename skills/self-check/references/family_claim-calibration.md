# Claim calibration

## What this family governs

Claim calibration is the distance between what was measured and what is asserted. It fails when a result on three negation probes becomes a claim that language models understand negation, when success in two of five cases is printed as "works 40% of the time", when a figure extrapolated from a one-hour pilot appears as 41.2 GPU-hours in the register of a measurement, and when a work package labelled high-risk promises in the next sentence to establish its outcome. The damage is not local to the sentence, because a reviewer who catches one inflated claim starts auditing the rest, and a citer who never reaches the experiments section carries the overclaim forward after the paper is forgotten. Authors are the last people who can see this. The author knows the evidence well enough to read the missing qualification back in silently, so the flat sentence and the calibrated one look identical to the one person revising them, and the drift arrives one word at a time over many passes where every individual step is a defensible tightening.

## The mechanism

A claim sentence carries two independent things. There is the proposition, and there is a marker of how strongly the proposition is being asserted. The markers are a graded vocabulary, and each one is a dial with a weak end and a strong end.

| Carrier | Weak end | Strong end |
|---|---|---|
| Modal | may, might, could | will, does |
| Status verb | suggest, indicate, appear, seem | demonstrate, establish, prove, confirm |
| Approximator on a quantifier | almost all, fairly complete, largely universal | all, complete, universal, unique, optimal |
| Frequency adverb | may occur | often, frequently, typically, always |
| Certainty intensifier | absent | obviously, clearly, of course, undoubtedly, trivially |
| First-person stance marker | we find, in our view, as far as we can tell | absent, so the claim reads as a fact about the world |
| Numeric form | 2 of 5, roughly 40 GPU-hours, a 95% interval | 40%, 41.2 GPU-hours, a bare point estimate |
| Scope phrase | on the three probes of Section 4 | absent, so the claim covers whatever its nouns name |

Now take the destructive move. Delete `may` from "vocabulary sharing may explain transfer". The sentence is still grammatical. Every referent is unchanged, no term has been substituted, a diff shows one deleted word, and by any mechanical test of meaning preservation nothing happened. It even reads better, because the hedge was the flabbiest word in the sentence. The dial nevertheless went to its maximum, and the evidence did not move with it. That is the whole family in one operation — the edit is invisible to every check an editor can run on the text, and it is a substantive change to what the paper claims.

The asymmetry is what makes this dangerous rather than merely annoying. Whether the strong form is true depends on evidence that lives outside the document, in the experiment log and in the author's head. The text alone cannot decide it. So detection is cheap and mechanical, and the repair is not available to anyone but the author. From that you can derive most of the rules yourself. Pick any carrier in the table, write the test as a literal search for its weak-end tokens, and then ask what changes if the token is removed. If the answer is that the claim now covers more cases, holds more confidently, or looks less noisy, the token was load-bearing and the correct output is a query showing the before and after side by side with a note saying which claim would become stronger. Run the same derivation backwards and you get the under-claiming rules, because a hedge sitting on a result the numbers fully support buries the contribution and reads as the author doubting their own data. Run it on the numeric row and you get the digits, percentage and interval rules, since a rounding decision is a claim-strength decision. Run it on the scope row and you get the population-versus-sample rules and the absolutes.

## The central tension, stated honestly

Confident prose is rewarded, and the evidence for that is better than the evidence for most writing advice. Qiu and colleagues analysed 2,439 NIH and 9,096 Novo Nordisk Foundation applications including rejected ones, with PI and year fixed effects and a 139-term validated dictionary, and found promotional density positively associated with funding at both funders with overlapping odds ratios near 1.5. Millar tracked 140 stance features across 901,717 NIH applications from 1985 to 2020 and found a consistent decline in weak possibility features and a rise in features conveying certainty, importance and empiricism. Wang replicated the direction on 12,187 Swedish Research Council abstracts. Grant prose has become measurably less tentative over forty years, and flat prose now sits out of register for the genre. The limits are as important as the finding. Most stance work analyses funded proposals only, so it describes the register of things that got funded and cannot show that the register caused the funding. Qiu is the exception that includes rejections, and even there the absolute margin is thin — 0.93 percent of words against 0.89 percent. That is a rule about the low tail. It is not a licence for florid prose, and above roughly twice the corpus mean the same dictionary describes hype that reviewers penalise.

The trade-off makes the guard against silent hedge deletion more necessary rather than less. Two facts sit on top of each other here. Confidence is rewarded by the funder, and unsupported confidence is the defect reviewers punish hardest — overclaiming was the second most frequent claim-calibration complaint in the ICLR sample behind unsupported claims. Both are true, so somebody has to price the trade for each individual sentence, and the only person who can is the one who knows what the experiment showed. An editor or a tool that deletes the hedge has not made the trade. It has made the claim stronger and left the author holding a liability they never saw. The guard is therefore not a preference for tentative prose. It is a rule about who decides. Raise every strength change as a query, let the author take the confident version wherever the evidence carries it, and the funder-facing incentive is fully available without any of it being borrowed.

## The rules, grouped by mechanism

Sixty-three rules. Thirty are decidable from the text alone, twenty-two are not, and eleven are corpus observations with no decidability verdict. Almost all are query-tier, which follows directly from the mechanism above.

### A. The guard and the query discipline (2 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| An edit deletes, weakens or replaces a hedge, drops an intensifier from a graded absolute, or removes a first-person stance marker | Never apply automatically. Show before and after side by side and state which claim becomes stronger. Even where the hedge is genuinely empty filler, the query is still the correct output | Local extension. Warranted by World Bank Style Guide A.1 and Editors Canada PES 2024 A13.1, since claim strength is meaning |
| Separate a statement of fact or a conclusion in the argument from a silly error in an incidental remark | Do not question the argument. Flag the incidental howler, the "Edmonton is about 5,000 kilometres east of Halifax" kind | Editors Canada, Ethical Editing of Graduate Student Texts, 2024 |

The guard has no counter-case, and that is deliberate. A hedge deletion may well turn out to be correct, and it is still never correct to apply it without the author seeing it. The query-discipline rule does have a live conflict — CIEP 5.4.5 asks the editor to raise any factual inaccuracy noticed, which reaches further into substantive claims than the Editors Canada line does.

### B1. Hedge density in both directions (7 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Several hedges modify the same claim in one sentence, so the level of commitment cannot be recovered | Report the hedges with their positions and ask which single level the evidence supports. Delete none | Williams (rev. Bizup), Style, 11th ed., Lesson 9 |
| A paragraph's main claim is flat while the only evidence it names is a single experiment, dataset or run | Report the mismatch and ask whether uncited support exists. Add no hedge, since adding one asserts a limit the document does not establish | Williams, Lesson 9 |
| A hedge sits on a claim the paper's own numbers fully support | Drop the hedge where the measurement is direct. Keep it where the inference is indirect | Corpus, do-llms-benefit-own-words |
| A specific weak epistemic feature carries a claim the evidence supports — check may, might, should, indicate, seem, possible, probable, perhaps one at a time | Replace with a certainty status verb or with `will` where the evidence supports it. Where it does not, keep the hedge and fix the claim | Millar 2024, Wang 2026 |
| A frequency adverb quantifies a result measured on one setup | Convert to a possibility claim, or state the setup the frequency was measured over | Corpus, do-llms-benefit-own-words |
| A tentative frame governs an applicant action that already appears in deliverables, milestones, budget or Gantt | Commit the action. Leave every modal on a finding, outcome or prediction exactly as written | Cronan & Deckard; Abbott |
| A hedged outcome has no matching entry in the risk section or contingency plan | Report the gap and ask whether to add a risk entry. Propose no change to the hedged sentence | Cronan & Deckard |

The Millar rule is the empirical one here. Population is 901,717 NIH applications from 1985 to 2020 across 140 stance features, replicated on 12,187 Swedish Research Council abstracts, with no outcome evidence attached — the finding is a trend in the genre rather than a measured effect on success. Millar's own warning is the counter-case. Trends must be read feature by feature, so a rule keyed to a whole LIWC tentative block will fire on features that moved the other way, and Wang found a hard-versus-soft-science split that matters across ERC panels. The likeliest error in this group is the action-versus-outcome confusion. "The effect may not persist at 70B" is a claim about an outcome and must keep its modal, because a committed future there promises a result the project cannot deliver.

### B2. Certainty markers, proof verbs and intensifiers (6 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Scan for obviously, clearly, of course, undoubtedly, it is clear that, needless to say, trivially | Report each with the statement it introduces. Propose no deletion, since the same word either marks a step a specialist may skip or marks where an argument was declined | Williams Lesson 9 Quick Tip; Halmos §10 |
| For each such statement, search the document for a proof, citation, appendix pointer or figure | Report the ones with no support found anywhere. Invent no pointer and leave the intensifier alone | Williams; Halmos |
| A correlational or probing result is introduced with a proof verb (establish, prove, confirm) | Use a verb that matches the evidence, such as demonstrate for a measured effect or find for an observation | Corpus, Instructions-shape-Production |
| Another paper's position is reported with a verb that grades it (assumes, merely) | Use a neutral reporting verb and put the disagreement in its own sentence | Corpus, Instructions-shape-Production |
| An intensifier or minimiser sits next to a number that already carries the size | Delete the qualifier and give the number its unit and scale instead | Corpus, Instructions-shape-Production |
| Wording is stronger than the result — superlatives, promotional adjectives, scope words the experiments do not reach | Rewrite to the narrowest true version, naming models, datasets and regime. Delete adjectives with no measurable content | ICLR 2024, ICLR 2025 reviews |

Two counter-cases bite hard here. Halmos allows `obvious` as a signal to a novice that a step is standard and skippable, which is why nothing in this group is deleted. A minimiser that relates two magnitudes must stay — "deviations of only 0.7 pp against a 12 pp effect" uses `only` to carry a comparison the bare number cannot, and the mined edit itself kept `only` while deleting `minor`. On the reviewer rule, a result that genuinely wins twelve of twelve benchmarks should say so, since reviewers score a contribution as incremental on the strength of the authors' own hedge.

### C. Scope, absolutes and priority (8 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative or superlative on an ungradable absolute — more or most or less applied to unique, optimal, exhaustive, universal, complete | Replace the whole phrase with the bounded comparative the evidence supports. Do not repair by deleting the comparative and leaving the bare absolute, which asserts more than the original | Abbott; Purdue OWL |
| An ungraded absolute (all, none, never, always, every, first, unique, optimal, complete) with no stated scope | Report each and ask for the scope that makes it true. Propose no wording, and never remove an approximator in the course of the check | Abbott; Purdue OWL |
| A priority marker (first, first-ever, novel, unprecedented, paradigm, only, no one has) with no prior work named | Name the closest prior attempt and the respect in which it falls short, or drop the marker and state the increment | ERC Guide for Peer Reviewers V6.0; WP 2027 |
| Novelty offered as its own justification — has never been done, there are no data on | Replace the absence claim with the consequence claim, and separately state what is novel and what is merely confirmatory | Kraicer, The Art of Grantsmanship (HFSP) |
| A claim in the abstract or introduction quantifies over a population broader than the sample evaluated | Narrow to the evaluated sample, naming datasets, model sizes and languages in the claim itself, or move the broad version into marked discussion | ACL Rolling Review R2 and R4; NeurIPS checklist item 1 |
| The abstract or title promises scope the body does not deliver, or omits the actual main result | Write the abstract last from the results tables. Every noun in it must appear in an experiment | ICLR 2024, ICLR 2025 reviews |
| A comparison to human performance names a capability rather than a task and its conditions | Restate as a task-and-conditions claim, naming the dataset, the label set, and that it holds under the test distribution only | Lipton & Steinhardt §3.4.1 |
| Claims or conditions stated informally enough that the reviewer cannot tell what was done | Replace every vague quantifier and unnamed component with the concrete value or name, and state claims as testable propositions | ICLR 2024, ICLR 2025 reviews |

The trap in this group is deleting the approximator. "Almost all languages in the sample" and "fairly complete" are hedged true claims that are already scoped, and stripping the approximator manufactures a false absolute out of a correct sentence. Two more worth holding. A benchmark or dataset release deliberately covers more ground than its experiments demonstrate, so narrowing the title to the experiments misdescribes the artifact, and on a negative-result paper taking the headline from the strongest measured number promotes a minor positive over the finding the paper exists to report. A claim that holds for a stated class where only one member was tested is repaired by a proof or a scope sentence rather than by substituting the instance.

### D. Numbers as claim strength (11 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A percentage with no denominator within one sentence, or over fewer than about twenty observations | Report the raw fraction for small samples, and state the denominator in the same sentence always | Zobel ch. 5, pp. 79-81 |
| Reported digits exceed any stated clock or apparatus resolution. Exclude counts, seeds, byte and parameter sizes, hashes, identifiers, step indices, quoted figures | Report the unsupported digits and ask for the timing method. Round nothing yourself | Zobel ch. 5 p. 79, ch. 11 p. 194 |
| A bare number is described elsewhere in the paper as an estimate, projection or extrapolation | Report the mismatch and ask the author to mark it as an estimate or confirm it was measured. Add no hedge on your own authority | Zobel ch. 5 p. 79 |
| A percentage change whose base value appears in neither this sentence nor the one before | Ask which value is the 100% base and have it named in the sentence. Do not infer the missing number | Zobel ch. 5 pp. 78-80 |
| "Orders of magnitude" in an abstract, results or contributions sentence | Ask for the measured multiplier in figures. Propose no substitution, since any figure you choose would be invented | Zobel ch. 5 p. 78 |
| A confidence interval obtained by doubling a standard error where n is below 10 | Use the t critical value for the actual degrees of freedom, and state the multiplier and n in the caption | Cumming, Fidler & Vaux 2007 Rule 5; Krzywinski & Altman 2013 |
| A significance or improvement claim resting on a threshold crossing alone, or the phrases highly significant, approached significance, trended toward | Report the effect with its uncertainty and its practical interpretation, drop the threshold language, and say plainly when a difference is real but too small to act on | ASA statement 2016 Principles 3, 5, 6; JAMA author instructions |
| A speed or cost win under roughly ten percent that does not rule out Zobel's seven rival explanations | Report the timing method and its resolution, interleave and repeat runs, state compiler flags for both systems, or reframe as parity | Zobel ch. 11 pp. 194-195 |
| A single coefficient stands in for a range that varies across the models tested | Attach the axis the number varies along in the same clause | Corpus, Do-LLMs-Play-Differently |
| A direction word applied to an outcome (improve, increase, reduce, enhance, speed up) with no magnitude | Attach a figure and the basis for it, or convert the claim into the measurement the project will make | Cronan & Deckard; Purdue OWL |
| A hedged comparative naming neither baseline nor amount | Name the quantity recovered and what it is recovered against | Corpus, Do-LLMs-Play-Differently |

The rounding rules are the ones that misfire. Full precision is correct when the digits are exact rather than measured, so counts, seeds, hashes, byte sizes and any figure quoted verbatim from a source are excluded, and rounding them destroys information. In the other direction, "0.5 second" is not equivalent to "half a second" — the first implies careful measurement — so neither substitution is safe without asking. A percentage is also the right form for a modest denominator when the reader needs to compare against other percentages, such as per-language accuracy across a benchmark suite, and there the answer is to give percentage and denominator together.

### E1. Measurement separated from interpretation (9 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A paragraph runs a measured result and an explanation for it together without marking which is which | Split so the measurement stands alone, then attribute the explanation as an assumption or tie it to the prior result that supports it | Corpus, Multilingual-Knowledge-Transfer |
| A section heading or opening sentence states the conclusion the section is about to test | Set up the question in the heading and let the result land where it is measured | Corpus, Multilingual-Knowledge-Transfer |
| The discussion converts a measured effect into a deployment benefit the paper did not measure | State the measured effect and its scope, and leave the downstream benefit to future work | Corpus, do-llms-benefit-own-words |
| A mechanism sentence proposes a cause in terms too vague to test | Drop the sentence or restate the mechanism as the specific constraint it implies | Corpus, Multilingual-Knowledge-Transfer |
| A statement has the grammatical form of a finding but rests on intuition | Move it to a section explicitly labelled motivation or conjecture, or add the words that mark its status in place | Lipton & Steinhardt §3.1 |
| A preliminary result is presented with a single interpretation | Add the strongest competing reading and the observation that separates them, then point at the aim that makes that observation | NIAID, Write Your Research Plan |
| The discussion presents every question it raised as addressed | Add explicit sentences naming what is left open, in limitations or at the end of the discussion | Lipton & Steinhardt §5.1 |
| A proposition carries no status marker — proved here, proved elsewhere, assumed, conjectured, unknown. Flag negative claims such as "p does not imply q" especially | Attach the status. For an unproved non-implication, say whether a counterexample follows, whether a reference exists, or that you do not know | Halmos §10 |
| A claim is stated without the figure, table, proof or citation that would establish it | Name the exact artifact in the same sentence, or weaken the claim to what the artifact shows | ICLR 2024, ICLR 2025 reviews |

Splitting is wrong when the paper measured the mechanism directly. "Accuracy drops 4 points when the shared tokens are removed, because removing them severs the shared representation" reports an ablation whose whole point was manipulating the cause, and splitting it demotes a causal finding to a guess. Two others. Speculation is valuable rather than merely permitted, and a paper with none has usually hidden its reasoning — the rule constrains where speculation sits and never whether it appears. A forward-looking introduction sentence previewing a result proved in Section 5 needs no artifact pointer, and bolting one on produces a citation stub for each promise.

### E2. Formal apparatus borrowing authority (4 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A claim that a quantity is small, large, close or bounded, reading as formal but carrying neither number nor theorem | State the theorem with its assumptions, give the measurement with its conditions, or rewrite as the informal observation it is | Lipton & Steinhardt §3.3 |
| A theorem, lemma or formal remark whose deletion would not change the headline claim and that you would not rely on to make a prediction | Move it to an appendix with a sentence saying what it does and does not cover, or cut it | Lipton & Steinhardt §3.3, §5.1 |
| An appeal to a named theorem whose hypotheses are not shown to hold in this setting, such as no-free-lunch licensing heuristics | State the hypotheses and why they hold here, or give the empirical reason for the design choice instead | Lipton & Steinhardt §3.3 |
| A hypothesis in a theorem statement that the proof never invokes, or an assumption the proof uses that the statement omits | Delete unused hypotheses and add used ones, so the statement stands alone | Knuth, Larrabee & Roberts §1; Halmos §11; NeurIPS item 3 |

The last rule is flagged unsafe in the ledger and should be run as a query only. Deleting a hypothesis the proof appears not to invoke can falsify the theorem, because implicit use is invisible to the check that flags it. A negative or delimiting result also belongs in the paper even when the headline claim does not rest on it, since its job is to stop a reader overgeneralising.

### F1. Consequence instead of activity or adjective (7 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| An objective whose approach text is only an inventory of methods, techniques, equipment or datasets | Add the clause naming what about the method defeats the thing that made the objective hard | ERC WP 2027 §1.6.5; Information for Applicants V11.0 §2.2 |
| The importance sentence carries only an evaluative adjective (crucial, key, fundamental, pressing, timely) | Replace the adjective with the blocked consequence, naming who is blocked and what they cannot currently do | ERC WP 2027 §1.6.5; Guide for Peer Reviewers V6.0 Annex 1 |
| The importance claim belongs to the field, disease or condition rather than to the proposed research | Rewrite so the subject is what this project will produce, and state what will be learned and how valuable that is | NIH Simplified Review Framework reviewer guide, Factor 1 |
| An objective phrased as an activity — investigate, explore, study, develop, examine — with no stated endpoint | Rewrite as the outcome plus its assessment criterion, keeping the activity as the method clause | NSF Merit Review Criteria |
| An understanding contribution with no sentence whose subject is the field's current understanding and whose verb is a change verb | Add one sentence naming the belief, method or bound that stops being true if the project succeeds, taken from the proposal's own related work, placed where the objectives begin | ERC Information for Applicants V11.0 §2.2 |
| A capability contribution with no sentence naming what becomes measurable or possible and who gains it | Add one sentence naming the capability, the question it makes answerable and the community that inherits it. Add no claim about beliefs changing | ERC Information for Applicants V11.0 §2.2 |
| Neither a change sentence nor any statement of which contribution type this is | Report the ambiguity, quote the two closest candidate sentences, and stop. Write neither sentence | ERC Information for Applicants V11.0 §2.2 |

The reason the last three are three rules rather than one is the counter-case. An understanding claim and a capability claim need different sentences, and manufacturing a theory claim for a corpus-building project produces exactly the overclaim a reviewer will test against the work plan. Where the belief only falls under one of two possible outcomes, name both, because a change sentence that quietly assumes the favourable branch reads as overclaiming to a reviewer who has just read the risk section. For the adjective rules, one clause is enough where the question is a famous standing problem or where the panel needs orienting to the field.

### F2. Commitment, risk and completeness (9 rules)

| Test | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A paragraph adds another strength while an obvious objection to the design, feasibility or evidence base is unaddressed anywhere | Stop adding strengths. Name the objection and dispose of it | Pier et al., PNAS 2018 |
| Promotional density runs well under roughly one term per four sentences, or falls away sharply after page one | Raise density toward roughly one validated term per four sentences and hold it steady across sections | Qiu et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024 |
| A load-bearing claim is assembled across separated sections and depends on the panel pooling its readings | Make each load-bearing claim complete at its own location. Restate rather than cross-reference where the claim carries weight | Pier 2018; Fogelholm 2012; Graves 2011 |
| Language deferring a decision, design choice or evidence to after the award, under a scored criterion | Make the decision now and state it with its fallback, or delete the claim that depended on it | Horizon Europe Part B preamble; NIH SRF introduction |
| A consideration the form asks applicants to address is omitted with no justification sentence | Add one sentence per omitted dimension naming why it does not apply, where the template asks | Horizon Europe Part B §1.2 |
| A claimed capability with no anchor in the work plan, resources section or applicant record | Add the pointer to the work package, named resource or prior result, or reduce the claim to what the anchors support | NSF Merit Review Criteria; JPL Levels of Edit VI-C.6 |
| A step labelled high-risk where the risk is in the outcome, whose outcome sentence has an unconditional verb | Rewrite the outcome as a measurement and name what is learned on each branch. Keep the risk label and every hedge | ERC Guide for Peer Reviewers V6.0 §1 |
| A step labelled high-risk where the risk is in schedule, access or resources | Say which kind of risk it is and add the timing mitigation. Leave the unconditional outcome verb alone | ERC Guide for Peer Reviewers V6.0 §1 |
| A high-risk label with the kind of risk unstated and an unconditional outcome verb | Report the pair and ask which kind of risk was meant. Propose no rewrite | ERC Guide for Peer Reviewers V6.0 §1 |

Three of these are empirical and carry their populations. Pier had 43 experienced reviewers evaluate 25 real NIH R01 applications in four simulated study sections, coding every strength and weakness in the written critiques — counts of strengths had no relationship to the numeric rating, while counts of weaknesses predicted it within a reviewer, so a positive rating reflected an absence of weaknesses rather than a large number of strengths. The same study measured an intraclass correlation of 0 for ratings of the same applications, meaning the data had the same structure as if 83 different applications had been used. Fogelholm found kappa of 0.23 for panel scores after discussion, no better than averaging two independent reviewers, and Graves estimated that 59 percent of 620 funded grants were sometimes not funded once random variability was taken into consideration. Qiu is the promotional-density study described in section 3, measured on 2,439 NIH and 9,096 Novo Nordisk applications including rejected ones, at 0.93 percent of words against 0.89 percent.

The counter-cases run the other way from the rules. Defect-first applies to the body and not the opening, since in a first-page pitch or scored lay summary the reader has not yet formed an objection and pre-emptive defect discussion reads as diffidence. Promotional density is a floor. It is not a target to maximise, and Reif-Lehrer's rule that every claim must be documentable still binds. Restating every load-bearing claim inflates length against a hard page limit, and redundancy between adjacent sections puts reviewers off. The schedule-versus-outcome split turns on one question — a delay severe enough to lose the data entirely is an outcome risk wearing a schedule label.

## What the corpus adds

Fifteen rules come from practice rather than from authorities. Eleven were mined from real revisions of this researcher's own papers, four of them marked as supervisor edits, and four were built from what ICLR 2024 and 2025 reviewers actually complained about. The reviewer frequencies are the useful part of the ranking. Unsupported claims appeared 9 times, and overclaiming, abstract-body mismatch and vagueness appeared 6 times each. That is one defect family producing 27 separate complaints. The mined edits mostly move the dial down by one notch. A real one, from Instructions-shape-Production 2918d8778, replaces `establish` with `demonstrate` in "We establish this through layer-wise probing of internal representations combined with attention-based interventions", and the same paper was edited twice in the same direction, down to a plain verb further along. From do-llms-benefit-own-words 27fca0e8d, `often outperforms` became `may outperform`. From Do-LLMs-Play-Differently e8322eb9f, a supervisor turned "achieve better performance to an extent" into "recover some of the performance", and elsewhere in the same commit `$0.92$` became `$0.92$ depending on the model`.

The corpus also contradicts any rule that always softens. Twenty-one hedge removals appear against a smaller number of hedge additions, and one commit runs `can improve` up to `improves`. The intensifier edit from Instructions-shape-Production 6fb5368a5 is the sharpest illustration of what mechanical softening would miss — "with only minor deviations of $\pm0.7$ toward later layers" became "with deviations of only $\pm0.7$ percentage points (pp) of probing accuracy toward later layers", so the vague word went and the comparative `only` stayed. The supervisor comments name the underlying ask directly. One reads "separate what is fact and what is speculation. IIUC We know for a fact the units are large but we assume this is the reason". Another rejects a sentence as "a bit too speculative but also vague? better drop or say something more exact".

## How to run the pass by hand

Work from evidence to prose, never the reverse. Begin with the results tables and the experiment log open, and write down for each headline claim the population actually evaluated, the number of runs behind it, and the artifact that establishes it. That list is the calibration target for the whole document. Then make one pass per carrier from the table in section 2 rather than one pass over the whole text, because a mixed pass turns into rewriting. Search for the weak-end tokens, then the strong-end tokens, and at each hit ask a single question — does the evidence on your list support this exact strength. Three outcomes follow. The evidence supports a stronger form, so strengthen it and say so in the revision note. The evidence supports a weaker form, so weaken it. The strength cannot be settled from what you have, so leave the wording untouched and write the query. Take the numbers next, since they are the carrier authors least suspect. For every percentage find the denominator, for every change percentage find the base, for every timing check the digits against the clock you actually used, and for every figure described elsewhere as a projection check that its register says estimate. Close with the two structural checks. Read the abstract against the tables and confirm every noun in it appears in an experiment. Then read the risk section against the outcome sentences and confirm the same uncertainty appears in both places, because a passage labelled high-risk whose outcome sentence promises to establish the outcome is a contradiction the reviewer resolves against you. Never make a strength change silently, including in your own students' drafts. The query with before and after side by side is the deliverable, and the author decides.

## Where this family is weak

The guard at the centre of the family is a single-source rule, and its source is this project. No free professional standard states it, which is exactly why every source-derived rule that touched hedging arrived unguarded and had to be repaired — eighteen of the sixty-three rules here are repairs of bundled unsafe rules. That is a real weakness. The warrant is an inference from World Bank A.1 and Editors Canada A13.1 plus five independent unsafe flags in this ledger, and inference is weaker than a published standard. The query-discipline rule has a source that another authority contradicts, since CIEP 5.4.5 reaches further into substantive claims than Editors Canada does. The theorem-hypothesis rule is flagged unsafe outright and can falsify a theorem if applied. Nine grant rules rest on the wording of specific funder documents, so they expire when the template does, and one is explicitly marked funder-conditional.

The evidence problem is the one to hold onto. Most stance research analyses funded proposals only, so it can describe what funded prose looks like and cannot show that writing that way causes funding. Qiu includes rejections and is the strongest study in the family, and its margin is 0.93 percent of words against 0.89 percent. Pier's reviewer study is stronger on internal validity and was run on simulated study sections rather than live ones, and its intraclass correlation of 0 undercuts the premise that reviewer response to prose is stable enough to optimise against at all. Twenty-two of the sixty-three rules are not decidable from the text, and eleven corpus rules carry no decidability verdict, so a third of this family depends on judgement that the page cannot supply.
