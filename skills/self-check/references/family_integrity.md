# The integrity family

## What this family governs

This family governs whether the document agrees with itself and whether everything it promises is actually there. Cross-references resolve to elements that exist, numbered sequences have no gaps or duplicates, the same quantity carries the same value in the abstract, the table, the figure and the conclusion, each citation supports the sentence it is attached to, and the apparatus a reader needs — a caption, a source line, a licence, a limitations section — is present. Failure looks like this. A reader following "As Figure 4 shows" in a three-figure paper loses their place mid-argument, and a referee who finds one headline number written four ways stops assessing the science and starts auditing the arithmetic. Zobel records exactly that failure in a real paper, where one figure appeared as "almost 200,000", "about 170,000", "173,000" and "173,255". The damage is not proportional to the defect. One found contradiction licenses a search for more, and every other number in the paper is discounted whether or not it was wrong.

## The mechanism

Every other family in this ledger needs an outside referent. Deciding whether a claim is overstated needs the experiment, deciding whether a sentence is clear needs a reader, deciding whether a budget is justified needs the funder's rules. This family needs neither. It compares the document against itself, so the only inputs are two places inside the same file plus the assertion that they ought to match. That is why a non-specialist can run this pass on a paper whose subject they do not understand, and it is the whole reason the family exists as a separate level of edit.

From that one property you can derive most of the family yourself. Start with the observation that every rule here is a **pairing** rule. Find any fact the document states twice and you have found a rule. A quantity in the abstract and in Table 1 (rule 1). A heading in the contents list and in the body (rule 40). A term in the prose and the same term baked into a plotting script (rule 2). A staff-month count in the methodology narrative and in the Resources section (rule 16). A symbol in a displayed equation and in the notation table (rule 37). None of these needs to know what the quantity means. Each needs only that the two copies exist and be compared.

Pairing then generalises to **presence**, because a pointer is half of a pair whose other half must exist. A reference to Figure 4 in a three-figure paper is an unpaired pointer. A figure the text never mentions is an unpaired target. Rule 38 asserts both directions in one test, and rule 59 asserts the same shape over a bibliography — every entry should have a citing sentence, every citation a resolvable entry. Missing apparatus is the degenerate case where the expected partner is not another mention but a required element, so rule 8 sweeps for the absence of source lines and captions rather than for wrong ones.

Now the hard part, and the reason almost none of this can be auto-fixed. Detecting that two halves disagree never tells you which half is wrong. The check has no access to the experiment, so when the abstract says 40 languages and Section 3 says 38 the comparison is complete and the repair is undetermined. Editors Canada makes this a rule of practice rather than a caution — recompute the total, then query with the arithmetic shown, because an arithmetic mismatch has at least two causes, a typo in the printed total and a missing row, and silently correcting the total picks one cause without evidence (rule 4). An editor who does that converts a visible error into an invisible one and destroys the author's only chance of noticing that the underlying data is wrong. The JPL formulation is blunter still. An integrity edit will not resolve any apparent inconsistencies or contradictions in the meaning expressed in different parts of the report (rule 47). Detection is mechanical. Resolution is authorship.

The exception tells you when a rule may fix rather than query. Where the mismatch has exactly one possible repair, the asymmetry vanishes and the edit is safe. An unmatched delimiter in `f(g(x, h(y))` has one closure (rule 45). A duplicate in the sequence Table 1, 2, 2, 4 has one renumbering (rule 39). A contents entry that disagrees with a body heading has an authoritative side by convention (rule 40). So the practical rule is that a mismatch with a unique repair is silent, and a mismatch with two or more candidate repairs is a query. That single distinction sorts this family into its two tiers and you can apply it to any new check you invent.

One more derivation, because it is where this pass most often does damage. Every counter-case in the family has the same shape. Two values that look like one quantity are frequently two quantities sharing a name. A raw corpus size beside the deduplicated size (rule 1). A table of test-set scores beside prose quoting the development set (rule 23). A published number beside your own reproduction of the same setting, where the gap between them is itself the finding (rule 35). A derivation assuming full-batch gradients beside a released script using batches of 128 for a different ablation (rule 34). In every one of those the correct repair differentiates the two names. Making the two numbers agree overwrites a correct value and deletes a result. So the test to run before every reconciliation is whether the two mentions measure the same thing, and if they do not, the fix goes into the labels.

Finally, a note on why reading does not substitute for this pass. Numbers and names drift because they are edited independently in several places, and each mention is locally plausible, so nothing on the page looks wrong at the moment of reading. Late renames propagate by find-and-replace, which by construction cannot touch a binary image or a separate plotting script. Numbering defects arise almost entirely from insertions and deletions made after the last careful read, so they are absent from every draft the authors actually studied. This family catches a class of error that attention cannot.

## What the corpus and the reviewers add

Nine rules were mined from real revisions of this researcher's own papers and grants, and four from defects ICLR reviewers complained about. The corpus rules are narrower and more embarrassing than anything in the literature, and that is their value. Two of them are simply the author's own in-line notes to himself, preserved as evidence that the defect was real. One reads `\[name]{update numbers so table1, figure3 and text all match}`, and the commit that resolved it replaced the note with the single settled value, `a \ceq score of 71.8\%`. Another shows a results table shipping with a live placeholder inside a real row, `Parallel sentences & \[name]{x.x} & \ceqbar{0} \\`, repaired to `1\% Parallel sentences & $-2.2\%$ & \ceqbar{0} \\`. A third catches prose left behind by a rerun, where `$\ceq[\text{WWT-Ar} \rightarrow \text{English}]=20.8\%$ while $\ceq[\text{English} \rightarrow \text{WWT-Ar}]=6.6\%$` became `17.9\%` and `7.3\%` after the numbers moved.

The grant-side corpus rules bite on identity rather than on results. An unfilled ORCID field shipped as `ORCID: \[name]{XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX}` where it should read `[orcid]`, and a CV line reading `[org] and [org] postdoctoral fellowships (2023, \[name]{both declined in favour of the [org]--IBM position? --- confirm})` became two precise lines, `[org] Postdoctoral Fellowship (2023, held); [org] Postdoctoral Fellowship (2023, awarded and declined)`. Notice that the author's own query note is what turned an ambiguous line into two exact ones. That is the query mechanism working inside a single author's head, and it is the same mechanism a supervisor supplies to a student.

One corpus rule was fixed by the supervisor rather than the author. A section carried `NOTE: Under Construction. This section needs solid improvement` in body text, and the supervisor deleted the line rather than updating it, which the rule records as the usual fate of status text left in prose. The reviewer rules carry no before and after pairs, only counts of how often the complaint appeared — eight reports of two passages asserting incompatible things, seven of uncited claims, five of erroneous equations, four of one quantity reported with different values. Those frequencies are the strongest evidence in the family that this pass is worth its time, because every one of those defects was findable without any domain knowledge and was still shipped.

## How to run the pass by hand

Run it last, after the science is settled and after every late rename, because the defects are created by the edits and not by the drafting. Work in three sweeps. The first is machine work, the second is comparison work, the third is reading.

Sweep one is a script. Numbering gaps and duplicates, pairwise-identical captions, unmatched delimiters, contents against body headings, undefined and unused cross-references from the build log, a grep for retired terminology across the tex, the plotting scripts and the SVG sources, a grep for placeholder shapes in identity fields and table cells, a character count on every portal box, a rendered check of font size and margins, a diff of the two abstracts, a per-item-type field check over the bibliography, and the list of bibliography entries no sentence cites. Every one of these has a unique repair or a pure report, so a script running them costs nothing and no judgement is spent.

Sweep two is a person with a spreadsheet and no need to understand the research. Pull every mention of each headline quantity into one row per quantity with its location, and look for rows whose cells disagree. Re-add every total, redo every conversion, recompute every percentage from its printed numerator and denominator. Match the methodology numbers against the budget line by line. For each disagreement, write the query with the arithmetic shown and stop. The single discipline that makes this sweep safe is refusing to pick a winner. Where you believe you know which value is right, you are using knowledge the document does not contain, so it belongs in the query rather than in the file.

Sweep three cannot be delegated to anyone but a domain reader, and this is where the honest budget goes. Open the sources behind the load-bearing citations and confirm each states the claim it is attached to. Have a co-author re-derive every displayed result from the stated assumptions. Cross the setup's list of conditions against the reported results and ask what was run but not shown. Check the closest published results to your aims are cited. Compare the released code against the method description. Nothing in this sweep is decidable from the text, which is why it is last and why it is the part that gets skipped.

## Where this family is weak

The largest weakness is thin sourcing on exactly the rules that look most authoritative. Six rules rest on a single 1980 JPL publication and nothing else — numbering sequences, duplicate captions, contents-against-headings, cross-references by title, delimiter matching, and the injunction against resolving contradictions. They are plausible and they are mechanical, and they have not been corroborated against a second authority in this ledger. Worse, five Editors Canada citations carry an explicit warning in their own source field that the 2016 standard cited was superseded with no verified 2024 equivalent, and one rule, the later-pass propagation check, rests entirely on superseded standards E3 and E4. Treat those as advice with a broken provenance chain and re-verify before quoting them to anyone.

Several single-funder rules are written as though they were general. The per-figure source-data mandate is a Science requirement, and the rule says so directly, adding that Nature has no equivalent. The under-100 citation ceiling is an NIAID number carrying NIAID's own "if possible" qualifier, so it is a signal to surface rather than a limit to enforce. Five rules invert between funders, which means their finding is not a property of the text at all. The same URL is permitted at one call and fatal at another. The same AI-disclosure sentence is mandatory under Horizon Europe and something UKRI tells its assessors to disregard. The same "in preparation" entry should be restated as a preprint under ERC and left alone under a funder that excludes preprints. A pass that fires those rules without first reading the call in front of it will do harm.

Then there are the checks that look decidable and are not. The cross-reference rule is flagged unsafe in the ledger for bundling — its fix offers "delete the orphaned element" beside "correct the pointer", and the destructive branch can remove legitimate content, so the two must be split before the rule is run. The internal-consistency rule is flagged unsafe for having no carve-out for quoted material, reference titles and proper dataset or model names, all of which must keep their original form. Number agreement is marked decidable and its counter-cases show four separate ways two correct values can look like one wrong one. Uncited-claim detection is marked decidable and its counter-case is that under an open review cycle the demand often resolves into padding the bibliography with the reviewer's own loosely related papers. Citation verification is marked decidable and the rule itself concedes that full verification does not scale to a hundred references under deadline. Nine of the corpus rules have no recorded decidability at all, which is honest and also means they have never been tested as automatable.

The deepest limitation is structural rather than a gap to be filled. This family compares the document against itself, so a document that is uniformly and consistently wrong passes every check in it. Propagate one incorrect number to every mention, cite one paper that does not support the claim in a bibliography that is otherwise flawless, and the whole pass returns clean. That is the price of the property that makes the family safe for a non-specialist, and no amount of additional rules inside the family can recover it.
