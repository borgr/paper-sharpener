# Habits of the drafting model

Not writing advice. These are failures observed in the model running this skill (Claude, 2026-10),
which a human editor or a later model may not share. Check every suggestion against this list
before showing it. Revisit the list when the model changes: delete an entry the model no longer
needs, and add one when the writer has to correct the same thing twice.

## Regenerating instead of editing

**Observed.** Asked to fix a rank or a verdict, the model wrote a new paragraph around the guide's
checklist and fitted the writer's content into it. Points the writer made were dropped
("still eager to improve"), bent (a comparison the writer rejected), or cut as hedges when they
carried a stance ("the number of papers matters less than the skills it shows").

**Check.** The Suggestion starts from the writer's sentences and changes only what the finding
names. Above it, list the passage's points one per line in the writer's terms, and tick each off
in the After. When a point's meaning is unclear, ask instead of proposing.

## Embellishing looked-up facts

**Observed.** "The field's reference benchmark" on an employer's page became "now used to
evaluate the safety of web agents"; "joined industry" became "top venues".

**Check.** Each fact in an After appears at the strength its source states, and the source is
named in the finding.

## Prose tics in suggestions

**Observed in the model's own Afters**, not in the writer's text:

- **Colons** introducing a clause or a list inside a sentence ("He is well ahead: he …"). Use a
  full stop, or a connective that names the relation (*because*, *so*, *yet*).
- **"And" joining two clauses with no stated relation** ("I have advised him for six months, and
  collaborators recommended him"). Name the relation (*on the recommendation of*, *yet*,
  *because*) or make two sentences. Lists of parallel items are fine.
- **Vacuous words** where a specific one exists: *what* ("what it shows"), *thing*, *aspect*,
  *matters*, *the level*. Name the noun ("the skills it reflects").

- **Staccato after cutting the above.** Removing colons and "and" left runs of short sentences
  that do not connect, each starting a new subject. Keep the reader's thread: repeat or refer to
  the subject ("In those months", "his skills"), or open with a linking word that names the
  relation.

- **Spelling drift.** Suggestions arrived in British spelling (*behaviour*, *criticised*,
  *organised*) inside a letter written in American spelling (*modeling*, *behavior*). Match
  the writer's spelling; when the letter mixes, make it consistent and list the change.

**Check.** Read each After for these five before showing it. Then list the first five words of
each sentence in the paragraph it sits in: each opener should pick up something the previous
sentence gave.
